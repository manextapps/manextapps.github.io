Matchstick

Matchstick puzzles with answers: move one match

Each equation below is wrong. Move exactly one match to make it true. You can play them right here, open the answer under each one, or print the whole set with a separate answer key.

By ManextApps5 min readUpdated 6 November 2026

Puzzle E1 (easy)

Move exactly one match to make the equation true.

Tap a match to pick it up. Dashed spots show where it can go, and the board tells you right away if the new equation is true. Hint goes in three steps: first it names the number or sign the match comes from, then it highlights the match, then it shows where it goes. With a keyboard, Tab to a match and press Enter, then Tab to a spot and press Enter again. Your solved puzzles are remembered in this browser, so you can come back later and carry on.

The rules

People argue about matchstick puzzles mostly because nobody says the rules out loud. Here they are, and the board above follows them exactly:

  • Move exactly one match. You can't add one, throw one away, break one or lay one diagonally.
  • Digits are drawn like a digital clock, with seven possible matches each. A 1 is two upright matches, and it may stand on either side of its space.
  • Signs are made of matches too. A minus is one match, a plus is two (one flat, one upright) and an equals sign is two flat matches. So a sign can change into another sign.
  • The result has to be a real equation: numbers and plus or minus signs, exactly one equals sign, and both sides equal. No numbers that start with a zero, like 05, and no sign at the very start.

Every puzzle on this page leads to exactly one true equation under those rules. I checked each one with a program that tries every possible move, so if you find a second answer, I'd honestly like to hear about it. The board accepts any true equation you make, not just the one in the answer key.

How to solve them

Here's the trick most people never notice: there are only a few ways a digit can change with one match. The table uses the usual 1 on the right side of its space. If you know them, you stop staring at the whole equation and start checking a short list.

A few things jump out. The 8 is the only digit that can give a match away to three different digits. The 1 and the 4 can't change shape by moving one of their own matches at all. And 0, 6 and 9 turn into each other by moving a single match, which is behind a lot of easy puzzles.

Signs work the same way. Take the upright match out of a plus and you get a minus. Put a spare match on a minus and you get a plus or an equals sign. Lay the upright match of a plus flat and the plus becomes an equals sign.

  1. Work out how far off the equation is.

    If 5 + 7 = 5 is off by 7, you need to lose 7 on the left or gain 7 on the right. That narrows the search a lot.

  2. Try a change inside one digit.

    Look for 0, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 9 first. They're the digits that can become another digit with a match of their own.

  3. Then try moving a match between two digits.

    One digit gives a match away (8, 9, 6 or 7 are the usual donors) and another digit takes it (0, 1, 3, 5, 6 or 9).

  4. Then look at the signs.

    Turning a minus into a plus needs a spare match from somewhere. Turning a plus into a minus frees one up to use on a digit.

  5. Last, swap the signs around.

    Moving one match from the equals sign to the minus swaps them. It looks like cheating, but it's a legal move and some of the hard ones need it.

Questions

Can a 1 be turned into a 7?

Yes, if the 1 stands on the right side of its space. A 7 is that 1 with a match across the top, so adding one match turns it into a 7, and taking the top match off a 7 leaves a 1.

Is it allowed to turn the equals sign into something else?

Yes, as long as the finished equation still has exactly one equals sign. Moving one match from the equals sign onto a minus swaps the two signs, and two of the puzzles here need exactly that.

What about answers like 6 + 4 ≠ 4?

Not here. A not equal sign needs a diagonal match, and the rules above don't allow diagonal matches. If you allow it, almost every puzzle gets a lazy second answer.

Why does my answer count when it isn't the one in the key?

The board checks the rules, not a stored answer. Every puzzle in this set has only one answer, but if you ever find another true equation, it still counts.

Is there an app with more of these?

Yes. My Matchstick Puzzle app for Android has 550 levels, including two match moves, add or remove puzzles and shapes, plus a daily puzzle. It follows the same rules as this page and works offline.

Can I check a puzzle from a book?

Yes. Type it into the matchstick equation solver and it lists every answer under these rules, for one match or two.

Can I use these in class?

Of course. Print the page and the answer key comes out on its own sheet. I only ask that you don't copy them onto another website.
