Dog legged staircase calculator

A dog legged stair climbs halfway, turns on a landing and comes back over itself. For a 10 foot floor height (3048 mm) with risers up to 190 mm and 250 mm treads, that's 17 risers in two flights and a stair hall about 4.0 m long and 2.1 m wide.

By ManextAppsUpdated 24 November 2026

Floor to floor is from the finished floor below to the finished floor above. Type 3048 mm, 3.05 m, 10' or 10' 0". A number with no unit counts as mm or inches, whichever you pick. The gap between flights can be 0 if the two flights share a wall.

Start from

The drawing is the stair seen from above, to scale. You start on the floor at the left, climb flight 1 to the landing on the right, turn around and climb flight 2 back to the upper floor. The thin lines are the tread edges.

Stair hall size for common floor heights

Every row takes the fewest risers that stay at or under the max, then splits them into two flights. When the count is odd, the first flight gets the extra one. The hall length is the longer flight plus a landing at each end, one in the middle of the turn and one on the floor where you step on and off. Both are as deep as a flight is wide.

A worked example: a 10 foot floor height

  1. Total rise. 10 feet is 3048 mm, from finished floor to finished floor.
  2. Count the risers. 3048 ÷ 190 is 16.04. You can't build part of a step, and rounding down would make every riser taller than 190 mm, so it's 17 risers of 179.3 mm.
  3. Split them. 17 doesn't halve evenly, so flight 1 gets 9 risers and flight 2 gets 8.
  4. Count the treads. Each flight has one tread fewer than risers, because you step off the last riser onto the landing or the upper floor. That's 8 treads, then 7.
  5. Flight length. 8 treads of 250 mm run 2.00 m. The second flight is 1.75 m.
  6. Stair hall. 2.00 m of flight, a 1.0 m landing at the turn and 1.0 m of floor to step onto make 4.00 m. Two 1.0 m flights and a 100 mm gap make the width 2.10 m.

Check the comfort with two risers plus one tread: 2 × 179.3 + 250 is about 609 mm. England's Approved Document K asks for 550 to 700 mm on a private stair, and this sits well inside it.

Why a 10 foot floor height turns into two flights

India's Model Building Bye Laws 2016 limit a flight to 12 risers (clause 4.17.4.1). A 10 foot floor needs 17 at 190 mm, so one straight flight isn't allowed. You need a landing somewhere in the climb.

You could keep it straight and put the landing in the middle. That works, but it's long: 15 treads of 250 mm plus the landing and the floor landing come to 5.75 m in a line, 1.0 m wide. Folding the same climb back on itself fits it into 4.0 m by 2.1 m, which suits the corner of a house much better. That's the main reason to pick a dog legged stair.

In the US the rule is different but leads to the same place. The IRC allows up to 151″ of rise between landings, so a 10 foot ceiling still fits one straight flight, if you have about 16 feet of floor for it, landing included. A U stair is what you use when you don't.

Floor height, not ceiling height

A stair climbs from one finished floor to the next. If 10 feet is your ceiling height, the stair also has to climb the slab and the floor finish above it, so the rise is more than 10 feet. Measure from the finished floor below to the finished floor above, where the stair will go, or add the slab and finish thickness from your drawings.

A few centimeters matter. At 190 mm risers, 3048 mm needs 17 risers, but 3240 mm needs 18. That's one more tread in the second flight, though the hall stays 4.0 m long. Past 3420 mm it takes 19, the first flight gets a tenth riser, and the hall grows to 4.25 m.

The limits to plan with

India's Model Building Bye Laws 2016, clause 4.17, give these for residential buildings:

  • Stair width at least 1.0 m, or 0.75 m for two storey row houses (4.17.2).
  • Tread at least 250 mm, measured without the nosing (4.17.3).
  • Riser at most 190 mm, and at most 12 risers in one flight (4.17.4.1).
  • Headroom at least 2.2 m in the stair, and in any passage under its landing (4.17.4.2).

Your state or city adopts its own bylaws, and the numbers there decide. For US homes the IRC sets a 7 3/4″ max riser, a 10″ min tread, a 36″ min width and 6′ 8″ of headroom, and a landing at least as wide as the stair. Both sets are presets above.

A lower riser climbs easier but needs more of them. At 165 mm, the same 10 foot floor takes 19 risers of 160.4 mm, two flights of 10 and 9, and a hall of 4.25 m instead of 4.0 m. A deeper tread does the same thing: 300 mm treads stretch the 190 mm stair to 4.40 m.

What fits under the landing

The mid landing in the 10 foot example sits 9 risers up, about 1.61 m above the floor. That's lower than the 2.2 m of headroom clause 4.17.4.2 asks for in a passage under a landing, so nobody can walk under it. The space under the first flight and the landing still takes a cupboard. If you need a door or a walkway under the stair, the landing has to be at least 2.2 m up plus its own thickness, which only happens with a much taller floor or a first flight with more risers than the second.

What this calculator doesn't check

It does the geometry: how many steps, how long, how much floor. It doesn't design the waist slab, the steel, the beams around the opening or the handrail, and it doesn't know your local bylaws. A concrete stair needs an engineer's drawing. Use these numbers to plan the room and to check that drawing makes sense.

Questions

How long is a staircase for a 10 foot floor height?

As a dog legged stair with 190 mm risers, 250 mm treads and 1.0 m flights, the stair hall is about 4.0 m long and 2.1 m wide (13′ 1 1/2″ by 6′ 10 1/2″). The longer flight itself is 2.0 m.

How many steps for a 10 feet height?

17 risers at a 190 mm max, each 179.3 mm, split 9 and 8. With a 7 3/4″ max riser and a 10 1/2″ floor added to a 10 foot ceiling it's also 17, each about 7 11/16″.

How many steps for a 9 feet height?

15 risers of 182.9 mm at a 190 mm max, in flights of 8 and 7. The stair hall is about 3.75 m by 2.1 m.

Is a dog legged stair the same as a U shaped stair?

Yes, mostly. Both turn 180° on a landing. "Dog legged" usually means the two flights sit side by side with little or no gap, while a U stair can have a wide open well between them. Set the gap above to match yours.

How wide should each flight be?

At least 1.0 m in a house under clause 4.17.2 of India's Model Building Bye Laws 2016, or 0.75 m in a two storey row house, and at least 36″ under the IRC in the US. Wider flights make the hall wider and the landings deeper, since a landing is as deep as the flight is wide.

Can the two flights have the same number of steps?

Only when the riser count is even. With 17, one flight has to have 9 and the other 8. You can add one riser to make it 18, which lowers every step a little, and the calculator shows that if you lower the max riser.
