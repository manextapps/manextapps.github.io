No spend challenge calendar

Pick the month, then tick each day you got through without spending on anything outside your essentials. The calendar counts your days and your streak, and keeps them in this browser.

By ManextAppsUpdated 6 November 2026

Tap a day to tick it, and tap again to undo. Days that haven't happened yet are locked, the same as in my app. Today doesn't break your streak until it's over. If you type what you'd normally spend on a day, the calendar adds up what you kept. Your ticks stay in this browser only.

How a no spend challenge works

For a set stretch of time, often a month, you don't spend money on anything that isn't essential. Before you start, write down what counts as essential for you: rent, bills, groceries, fuel, medicine, whatever you can't skip. Everything else waits.

Every day you get through without a non essential purchase is a no spend day. Tick it. The point isn't a perfect month. It's noticing how often you'd normally spend without thinking, and keeping that money instead.

Set your own rules first

The challenge only works if the rules are clear before day one, so decide these up front:

  • Your essentials list. Write it down and stick to it. Anything missing from the list waits until next month.
  • Planned bills. Payments already due, like a subscription renewal, usually don't count. Cancelling the ones you don't use is fair game, though.
  • Gifts and shared costs. Decide now what you'll do about a birthday or a group dinner, so it doesn't catch you out.
  • Where the money goes. Pick a goal for what you keep, and move it there at the end of each week.

The blank printed month has space for your essentials list and your goal at the bottom.

How long should it be?

A first try works well as a weekend or a single week. A whole month is the classic version, and it's long enough to break a habit like a daily coffee out or browsing shops online. Some people do a set number of no spend days in a month instead, say 15 of 30, which leaves room for normal life.

The calendar works for any of these. Tick the days that count, and ignore the rest.

Things that make it easier

  • Plan meals for the week before you shop, so the essentials trip is the only one.
  • Unsubscribe from shop emails for the month, and log out of the apps you buy from.
  • Keep a list of things you wanted to buy. If you still want them at the end, they were worth it.
  • Set aside a fixed amount on each no spend day, so you can see the money pile up.

None of this is financial advice. It's a habit with a calendar, and the habit is the part that pays.

Questions

What counts as spending in a no spend challenge?

Anything that isn't on your essentials list. Most people keep rent, bills, groceries, transport and medicine, and cut eating out, shopping, takeaway coffee and online orders.

Can I tick a day in the future?

No. Future days are locked until they've happened, so the calendar only shows days you really got through.

Does a slip ruin the month?

No. Leave that day unticked and carry on tomorrow. Your best streak is still there, and the month total is what counts.

Is my calendar saved?

Only in this browser, one month at a time. Clear the site's data or switch devices and it starts empty.
