Decimal feet to feet and inches

11.65 feet is 11′ 7 13/16″ to the nearest 16th. Type decimal feet, feet and inches, or plain inches, and the other two fill in, with the math written out so you can do the next one by hand.

By ManextAppsUpdated 24 November 2026

Decimal feet to feet and inches, by hand

Take 11.65 feet. The 11 is whole feet, so keep it. Everything after the point is a part of a foot, and a foot is 12 inches:

  1. Inches. 0.65 × 12 = 7.8 inches.
  2. Fraction. The 0.8 is part of an inch. Times 16 it's 12.8, which rounds to 13, so 13/16″.
  3. Together. 11′ 7 13/16″. That mark is 0.0125″ longer than 7.8″, far less than anyone can see on a tape.

The same three steps work for any size. 5.75 feet is 5′ 9″ exactly, because 0.75 × 12 is a whole 9. And 0.42 feet is 5.04 inches, so 5 1/16″.

Watch out for rounded decimals. 8.33 feet looks like 8′ 4″, since a third of a foot is 4 inches. But 0.33 × 12 is 3.96, so 8.33 feet is 8′ 3 15/16″, a 16th short. If a plan says 8.33 and means a third, use 8′ 4″. If it's a measured number, trust the 3 15/16.

Feet and inches to decimal feet

Going back is one division. Turn everything into inches, divide by 12, and add the whole feet:

  • 3 inches is 3 ÷ 12 = 0.25 feet, a quarter of a foot.
  • 2 inches is 2 ÷ 12 = 0.1667 feet, and 4 inches is 0.3333.
  • 7 13/16″ is 7.8125 ÷ 12 = 0.6510 feet, so 11′ 7 13/16″ is 11.651 feet.

Most inch sizes give a decimal that never ends, because 12 has a 3 in it. Whole inches only come out clean in multiples of 3: 3″ is 0.25, 6″ is 0.5 and 9″ is 0.75. Everything else gets rounded, and that's why decimal feet are usually written to two or three places.

Two numbers worth remembering: 0.01 feet is 0.12 inches, about 1/8″, and 0.1 feet is 1.2 inches, just over 1 3/16″. The tenths go 1 3/16, 2 3/8, 3 5/8, 4 13/16 and then 6 inches at 0.5, and the second half repeats the pattern from 6 inches.

Where decimal feet come from

Surveyors and civil engineers measure in decimal feet, so site plans, elevations and grade stakes use them. A finished floor at 101.25 is 101 feet 3 inches above the site's reference point. Builders measure in feet, inches and 16ths, so the numbers have to be converted somewhere between the plan and the saw.

One more detail if you work with old survey data. The US survey foot, a hair longer than the regular foot, was retired at the end of 2022. The difference is two parts in a million, about 3.2 mm over a mile, so it never matters for a board, but it can matter on old coordinates.

Questions

How do I convert 11.65 feet to feet and inches?

Keep the 11 feet. 0.65 × 12 = 7.8 inches, and 0.8 × 16 = 12.8, which rounds to 13. So it's 11′ 7 13/16″ to the nearest 16th.

What is 2 inches in decimal feet?

0.1667 feet. 2 ÷ 12 is 0.16666…, which rounds to 0.17 at two places.

What is 4 inches in decimal feet?

0.3333 feet, a third of a foot. At two places it's 0.33.

What fraction of a foot is 3 inches?

A quarter: 3/12 reduces to 1/4, which is 0.25 feet.

How many inches is 0.1 feet?

1.2 inches, which is 1 3/16″ to the nearest 16th.

Can I add and subtract feet and inches here?

This page only converts. For sums like 11' 7 13/16" + 2' 4 1/2", use the feet and inches calculator. It also takes decimal feet with a foot mark, like 11.65'.
