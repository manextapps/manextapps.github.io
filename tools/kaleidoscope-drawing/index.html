Kaleidoscope drawing

Draw one line and watch it repeat around the center, again and again, until it turns into a flower. Pick how many mirrors you want, draw with a finger or a mouse, and save the result as a picture.

By ManextAppsUpdated 9 November 2026

Ink

Your drawing stays in this browser, so it's still here if you come back later. Ctrl+Z undoes a stroke, and Ctrl+Shift+Z brings it back.

How to draw one

  1. Pick how many mirrors you want. 6 or 8 is a good place to start.
  2. Draw a short line near the center, and you'll see it repeat all the way around.
  3. Add a few curves further out, in a second ink, so the shape grows in rings.
  4. Switch off the guides to see it clean, then press Save as PNG.

You don't have to draw anything that looks like something. Loops, zigzags and dots all turn into patterns once they're repeated. Small strokes near the center make a tight flower, and long ones toward the edge make big petals.

Mirror on or off

With Mirror on, every slice is the mirror image of the one next to it, the way the pattern looks inside a kaleidoscope. Each stroke shows up twice in every slice, so 8 mirrors give you 16 copies. With Mirror off, every slice is the same stroke turned around the center, which gives a pinwheel that seems to spin.

The setting you pick doesn't change what's already drawn. It applies to the next stroke, so you can mix both in one drawing. Try a pinwheel in the middle and a mirrored ring around it.

How many mirrors, and what you get

The Mirrors number is how many slices the circle gets cut into. Each stroke is copied once per slice, and twice per slice with Mirror on. Here's what each setting does to one stroke:

Even and odd numbers feel different, and it took me a while to see why. With an even number, every stroke has a twin straight across the center, so the pattern looks like a wheel. With an odd number like 3 or 5, nothing sits directly opposite, and the pattern reads as a star with points.

One line, three results

Set Mirrors to 6 with Mirror on and draw a short straight line out from the center, tilted a little away from a guide. You get 12 lines in 6 narrow V shapes, and each V opens around a guide.

Now undo and draw the same line exactly along a guide. You'll only see 6 lines, because a line on a mirror is its own reflection and the two copies land on top of each other. The same happens halfway between two guides. Those are mirror lines too, they just aren't drawn.

Turn Mirror off and try once more. The 6 copies all lean the same way, like the blades of a fan.

The two kinds of symmetry

Mirror off gives rotational symmetry. Turn the finished picture by one slice, 45° with 8 mirrors, and it lands exactly on itself. In math this is called cyclic symmetry, of order 8 in that case.

Mirror on adds reflection. With 8 mirrors there are now 8 lines through the center, and you could fold the picture along any of them and the two halves would match. That's dihedral symmetry, and it's what a real kaleidoscope makes with two mirrors set at an angle. Two mirrors at 45° show 8 copies of whatever sits between them, the same as Mirrors 4 here with Mirror on.

Snowflakes are the classic six way example from nature. If you want a drawing that looks like one, use 6 mirrors with Mirror on and draw short branches off a line that runs along a guide.

A few ideas to try

  • Go up to 24 mirrors and draw tiny dots. It'll look like lace.
  • Drop to 2 or 3 mirrors and draw something bold. It'll look more like a butterfly or a logo.
  • Use black paper with yellow, pink and white ink for a neon look.
  • Draw with a thick brush first, then add thin lines on top.

Questions

How do I save my drawing?

Press Save as PNG. You get a 2048 by 2048 picture with the paper color behind it and no guides or watermark.

Does it work on a phone or tablet?

Yes. Draw with your finger or a stylus. The page doesn't scroll while your finger is on the drawing, so your line goes where you meant it.

Will my drawing be here tomorrow?

Yes, in the same browser. The page keeps your last 400 strokes and the paper color. Very long drawings lose their oldest strokes when you reload, so save a PNG of anything you want to keep.

Can I undo a clear?

Yes. Clear puts every stroke on the redo list, so pressing Redo brings them back one by one.
